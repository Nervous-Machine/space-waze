LEO Density — the drag grid

The question: when the Sun acts up, the upper atmosphere swells and satellites feel more drag. Which solar and geomagnetic drivers move the air in each specific place — and how sure are we?

What we measured

Air-density response through 40 storms (2021–2025), read from the accelerometers and GPS navigation residuals of two independent satellite fleets that never shared a model: GRACE-FO (460–510 km) and ESA Swarm (420–460 and 510–550 km) — 24 million Swarm measurements across 48 cells, learned one storm at a time with the answer hidden (leave-one-storm-out).

What we found

CERTIFIED (61 sectors): in a third of the storm sectors we can name the drivers that move the air, with most of that learned weight confirmed by repeated correct prediction — and a deliberately scrambled decoy signal correctly earns nothing there.
The sky-map replicates: where the learner beats simple references is not random — dawn and midnight sectors win, noon does not — and the same pattern appeared on the second fleet, which the model had never seen. Where the learner is not confident, its abstentions correctly flagged its own worst cells before they happened.
What we do not claim: a head-to-head win over the standard density model (MSIS) once that model gets the same local calibration any operator applies. We tested that claim ourselves and it did not survive — so it is not on the map. What is ours is the driver attribution and the where-the-reference-is-blind map.

Program history

This grid grew out of the original LEO density campaign (GRACE-FO era) — the poster, the method essays, and the first roadmap are preserved at the LEO program page.

Data sources

GRACE-FO accelerometer-derived densities (TU Delft TOLEOS) · ESA Swarm A/B/C · NASA OMNI drivers · storm-fold definitions and every threshold pre-registered.

Receipts

Every claim on this page comes from a pre-registered analysis: the hypothesis and its pass/fail rule were committed to a ledger before the data was touched, results were reproduced bit-for-bit, decoy signals ran as tripwires, and refused hypotheses stay on record. The complete gap register — data sources, refused hypotheses, and open staked predictions — is published alongside this atlas (how to read it).
