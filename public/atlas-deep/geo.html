Deep dive · Geostationary · 36,000 km

Geostationary | electron weather.

The question: at 36,000 km, “killer electrons” charge spacecraft surfaces. Can we learn what pumps them up, per local-time sector, well enough to plan around?

What we found.

Measured

The calm-side machine works.

Faster solar wind reliably pumps electron flux about two days later, in all four local-time sectors — and the ordering (midnight sector strongest, noon weakest) came out exactly as we staked it in advance. That is the shape spacecraft-charging planning needs.

Not claimed

What we do not claim.

The amplitude. Direction and ordering are earned; the size of the response is not yet confirmed to our bar, and we say so rather than round up.

Data sources.

Receipts.

Every claim on this page comes from a pre-registered analysis: the hypothesis and its pass/fail rule were committed to a ledger before the data was touched, results were reproduced bit-for-bit, decoy signals ran as tripwires, and refused hypotheses stay on record. The complete gap register — data sources, refused hypotheses, and open staked predictions — is published alongside this atlas (how to read it).
