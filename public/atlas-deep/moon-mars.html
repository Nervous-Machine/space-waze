Deep dive · 384,000 km → 1.5 AU

Cislunar · Moon · Transit · Mars.

The question: beyond the magnetosphere, radiation is the constraint. What do we actually know about dose at the Moon, on the way to Mars, and at Mars — and what is still guesswork?

The Moon — 17 years of measurements.

Certified

Earth’s alerts are not enough at the Moon.

We found solar-particle events that produced order-of-magnitude dose responses at the Moon while Earth’s official catalogs stayed silent — missed by threshold (too small at Earth), by latency (arrived at the Moon first), and by curation (never entered the record). Each was confirmed by an independent spacecraft in lunar orbit. If you operate near the Moon, you need lunar-vicinity monitoring, not just Earth-side alerts.

Certified

This solar cycle is different.

Counting the same way across 17 years, the quiet-day radiation bursts that seeded those discoveries are overwhelmingly a current-cycle phenomenon — 44 of 60 burst-days live in 2023–2025, and 2012 (a loud year by Earth standards) has zero. Models tuned on past cycles will miss this Sun.

Measured

The storm/quiet split holds.

One structural change — letting storm hours and quiet hours carry separate weights — fixed a 18× storm-hour error and stayed fixed across the entire archive. The magnetotail’s effect on dose still behaves opposite to textbook expectation; that stays an open question, published, not hidden.

Mars — two years, measured on the ground.

Certified

The atmosphere filters the flood.

Solar-particle events suppress surface dose at Mars — the same direction in both measured years — and in one season we certified the effect as essentially zero, firmly. (A confident zero is real knowledge: it bounds the hazard.) The same solar events push dose up at the Moon: same Sun, opposite signs, ordered by shielding. One rule, planetary bodies as its coordinates.

Certified

Two uncataloged storm arrivals at Mars.

Our residuals flagged them, and upstream plasma data from an orbiting spacecraft confirmed both, to the day — arrivals no official catalog lists.

Open mystery

The best open mystery.

A seasonal dose signal at Gale Crater appears in both measured years and has survived every explanation we tested — it is not calibration, not daily storm bookkeeping, not maskable. It waits on a dust-opacity data release or a Mars upstream monitor; our prediction for the ESCAPADE mission (~2028) is already staked in advance.

The two cheapest big holes.

gap what closes it
Earth→Mars corridor: crew transit dose has essentially never been continuously measured — 4 one-way snapshots in 25 years a small hosted dosimeter on any Mars-bound rideshare, hourly, one solar rotation; analysis pre-committed on our side
Lunar surface: the measurements exist (the LND instrument on the farside) but archive access is blocked one data-access agreement — and it matters twice: 17 years of lunar-orbit data hold only 46 magnetotail-storm days, so a farside stream is the only way to close that cell

Data sources.

LRO/CRaTER dose rates (UNH, full mission 2009–2026) · MSL/RAD surface dose (NASA PDS, two eras) · MAVEN SWIA/MAG upstream plasma · GOES proton/electron feeds (three instrument eras) · NASA OMNI · NASA DONKI arrival catalogs. Every fetch sha-pinned; the one gap in the archives themselves (an 8-month proton-coverage hole in 2020 between satellite generations) is documented in the register.

Receipts.

Every claim on this page comes from a pre-registered analysis: the hypothesis and its pass/fail rule were committed to a ledger before the data was touched, results were reproduced bit-for-bit, decoy signals ran as tripwires, and refused hypotheses stay on record. The complete gap register — data sources, refused hypotheses, and open staked predictions — is published alongside this atlas (how to read it).
